The
Manifesto

Owning software shouldn’t be a luxury.

The going rate

Somebody is paying $7.99 a week to delete duplicate photos from their own phone. That’s $415 a year for something the phone does by itself. This is what the top apps in a few very simple categories charge right now.

  • $7.99/ week

    to find duplicate photos on your own phone

    $415 a year

  • $14.99/ week

    to turn a photo of a page into a PDF

    $779 a year

  • $6.99/ week

    to change the channel on your TV

    $363 a year

  • $14.95

    to unlock a QR code reader

    your camera reads them free

Prices listed on the US App Store, checked 9 November 2026. Weekly plans × 52 weeks.

The Sevens

The fine print, in the same size type as everything else.

  1. 7

    apps

    Seven small apps to start. Each one replaces something that charges you rent.

    Fine print

    Seven is where we start, not where we stop. We keep going as long as there are subscriptions worth retiring.

  2. 7

    engineering days

    Each app gets about seven days of engineering. Small on purpose, and done when it does its job.

    Fine print

    That’s effort, not a stopwatch. One app takes three days, another takes ten. Seven is the budget we scope to, and the build log says when we blow it.

  3. 7

    day trial

    Use the whole app free for a week. When the week ends, it asks. It never bills you on its own.

    Fine print

    The clock starts when you install. No sign-up, no card up front, no charge at the end you didn’t choose.

  4. 7

    dollars. once

    Pay once, keep it, share it with your family. No weekly fee, and no “Pro” tier waiting behind the paywall.

    Fine print

    $7 is the US price. Where seven of your currency is worth more, it’s that: €7, £7. Where $7 is a lot of money, it costs less. On iPhone, Family Sharing is on: one purchase covers your whole family group, up to six people.

  5. 7

    month updates

    Every app gets at least one update every seven months, for as long as we’re around.

    Fine print

    That cycle is for chores: new OS versions, toolchain bumps, the dull work that keeps an app alive. Critical bugs and security fixes ship when we find them, not when the calendar says. New features are a bonus, and supporters can help fund them. Whatever they fund ships to everyone who owns the app.

Done means done

Each app does its one job, then stops growing. None of them will ever have any of this.

  • No subscriptions. Not weekly, not yearly, not “optional”.
  • No ads. You paid. That’s the business model.
  • No accounts. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to log into.
  • No servers. We don’t run a backend for your phone to call, so there’s no monthly cost to pass on to you.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no third-party code. We collect nothing.
  • No second paywall. $7 buys the whole app, not the right to see the next price.

Before you buy

Every app gets a page that says what your phone already does built in, then what we add on top. If the built-in is enough, keep your seven dollars. We’d rather you leave knowing than pay and wonder.

Pay for what you need.
Not forever. Just once.
